# Mounting the sign

Options for getting a TRMNL X onto a wall. None of these have been tried — the device has not arrived — so treat the whole page as unreviewed notes rather than a recommendation.

This is about the physical object, not the code. Nothing here affects the Worker.

## Constraints

The X weighs 365-450g, which is light enough that almost anything holds it. Only one requirement really shapes the choice:

- **It has to come off to charge.** Anything permanent means unplugging a wall fixture instead of lifting a sign down.

**Modifying the case is fair game.** The X is a DIY device and TRMNL make it easy to open for exactly this kind of customisation, so opening it, and drilling it if that turns out to be the best answer, are both on the table. Prefer the least invasive option that actually works — but do not pick a worse mount to avoid a hole.

**The magnet that ships with the X is not sufficient.** It is weak, and it is a single pad in the centre — so the sign hangs off one point and can rotate about it or peel from a corner. Any magnetic solution here means adding to it, not relying on it.

## Options

**Magnets in all four corners, inside the case.** The strongest version of the intended behaviour rather than a workaround for it, and the only option that keeps lift-off-to-charge exactly as designed. Open the case, seat a strong disc magnet in each corner, and put a steel plate — or four washers on the same centres — on the wall.

Four corners rather than more magnets in the middle, because the stock pad's problem is geometry as much as strength: a single centre point is a pivot, so the sign can rotate out of level and peel from an edge, and no amount of extra force at that one point fixes either. Corners resist rotation and hold the panel flat, and they put the holding force where peeling starts.

Use steel on the wall rather than opposing magnets. Magnet-to-magnet is stronger, but polarity then has to be mirrored correctly across all four positions or the sign fights you or only mounts one way round. Magnet-to-steel has no orientation to get wrong and is already far more than 450g needs.

**Nobody has opened one of these**, so corner clearance, what the bezel hides, and where the antenna and battery sit are all unknown until the device is in hand. Two things that are *not* the worry: E Ink is electrophoretic, driven by an electric field, so magnets near the panel do not disturb the image, and the extra corner mass is irrelevant at this weight. The real risks are physical clearance, adhesive that has to survive getting warm, and detuning the WiFi antenna by seating metal against it.

If clearance is tight, a shallow pocket drilled into the inside face of the back shell buys depth without breaking through. **Check the hidden-magnet design in [usetrmnl/mounts](https://github.com/usetrmnl/mounts) first** — if it achieves the same corner geometry from the outside, it saves the teardown entirely.

**Cleat or bracket screwed to the back shell.** Sits nearly flush, holds far more than 450g, and lifts straight up and off. Screwing into the shell rather than bonding with VHB is the better version now that holes are allowed: it is genuinely reversible, it does not care how warm the back gets, and it avoids committing to an adhesive that is a real problem to remove if the mount ever changes. Drill into the shell only — depth-stop the bit, and open the case first to see what is behind each hole.

VHB 4941 remains the no-holes fallback if a teardown turns out to be a bad idea.

**A printed surround that doubles as the mount.** Print a shroud the X drops into, with keyhole slots in the shroud rather than the case. The real Connectpoint units have an angled sun hood over the top and sides — reproducing that in black filament would make it read as a transit fixture rather than a tablet on a wall. Best-looking outcome by a distance, and the most work.

**Print an existing design** from [usetrmnl/mounts](https://github.com/usetrmnl/mounts) — the mainframe one, or the hidden-magnet one noted above.

**Industrial Velcro.** Ugly, five minutes, fully reversible, and genuinely fine behind a device that covers it.

**Shadow box frame.** Mount the X inside a shallow picture frame and hang it conventionally. Reads as a deliberate object.
